# AD-157 — The floor reads Windows shell verbs for every host; Antigravity closes its wiring routes through an optional port

- **status**: active

## Decision

1. **Windows shell verbs and forms enter the floor, for every provider.** They live in `src/core/floor/`, not in
   an adapter: a cmdlet that deletes outside the project is the same risk in a Cursor, Claude or Antigravity
   event, and the floor is shared. `src/core/` and `src/contracts/` still carry no vendor name. Added, by rule:

   | Rule | Names and forms added | Already covered before |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `outside-project-destruction`, `unprovable-destruction`, `wiring-tamper` by destruction (the target decides) | `remove-item`, `ri`, `del`, `erase`, `rd`, `clear-content`, `clc` | `rm`, `rmdir`, `dd`, `shred`, `truncate`, `mkfs*` |
   | `outside-project-destruction` with any argument (the target is a volume or disk) | `format-volume`, `clear-disk`, `format`, `format.com` | — |
   | `secret-access` by shell reader | `get-content`, `gc`, `type` | `cat`, `head`, `tail`, `less`, `more`, `od`, `strings`, `xxd`, `base64` |
   | `machine-control` | `stop-computer`, `restart-computer` | `shutdown`, `reboot`, `halt`, `poweroff` |
   | `unprovable-execution`, fetching side | `invoke-webrequest`, `iwr`, `invoke-restmethod`, `irm` | `curl`, `wget`, `aria2c`, `fetch`, `http`, `httpie`, `https` |
   | `unprovable-execution` and `unprovable-destruction`, text-executing side | `invoke-expression`, `iex`; `powershell`/`pwsh` with a `-Command` prefix, with `-EncodedCommand` (any parameter prefix) or positional; `cmd` with `/c`, `/k` or `/r`; script block; subexpression | `bash`, `sh` and the other shells with `-c`; `eval`, `source`, `.` |
   | wrappers (the real head comes after) | `start-process`, `saps`, `start` | `command`, `doas`, `env`, `nice`, `nohup`, `sudo`, `time`, `xargs` |

   `Remove-Item` is destructive with or without `-Recurse` and `-Force`; the target decides. The definitions the
   rules use:

   - **Normalized verb name**: remove every backtick and `^`; *grouping* — while the text holds `(` and what
     precedes the first `(` is empty, `$`, `@`, `.` or `&`, keep what follows it, drop `)`, `'` and `"`, and keep
     the first non-empty piece split on space and tab; cut at the first `(`; keep what follows the last `/` or
     `\`; lower-case; drop a trailing `.exe`.
   - **First name of a text**: replace `$(` and `@(`, then `(` and `)`, with a space; drop `"` and `'`; skip every
     piece that is exactly `&` or `.`; strip the leading `&` of the chosen piece; normalize it.
   - **Split form**: an `iex` segment whose last word ends in `(`, followed — through any separator — by a
     segment whose head is a fetch verb, denies `unprovable-execution`.
   - **PowerShell parameter prefix**: `-`, `/`, U+2013, U+2014 and U+2015 as the first character of a
     `powershell`/`pwsh` argument. **Unicode parameter dash**: in the membership and operand tests of the added
     verbs, a word starting with U+2013, U+2014 or U+2015 is treated as a parameter. Both come from the
     PowerShell source; neither was measured on this machine.
   - **Head**: the word the floor already picked (first non-wrapper word without a leading `-` and without `=`),
     with `start-process`, `saps` and `start` as wrappers. After `start` the floor also skips empty words, words
     matching `^/[A-Za-z]+$` and the value of `/d`, `/node` and `/affinity`; a first remaining word that starts
     with a quote makes the next word a candidate too, and any denying candidate denies.
   - **Operand after grouping**: when grouping changed the head word, a trailing `)` leaves every following
     operand.
   - **Pipeline**: segments joined only by a single `|` outside quotes.
   - **Text-execution form** and its **executed text**: (a) `invoke-expression`, `iex`, `eval`, `source`, `.`;
     (b) a POSIX shell with `-c`; (c) `powershell`/`pwsh` with a `-command` prefix of at least two characters or
     a `-command…` word; (d) with `-e`, `-ec` or an `-encodedcommand` prefix of at least three characters,
     decoded as base64 UTF-16LE, a word that does not decode denying; (e) positional `powershell`/`pwsh`, every
     suffix starting at a word without a parameter prefix; (f) `cmd` with `/c`, `/k` or `/r`; (g) **script
     block**: every `{`, at any position of a word that does not start with a quote, up to the next `}` or the
     end of the segment; (h) **subexpression**: every `$(` in any word, and `@(` or `(` at the start of an
     unquoted word after the head, up to its matching `)`.
   - **Sub-segment**: each segment the existing shell tokenizer makes of an executed text. Only the head of each
     sub-segment is compared with the destructive, machine and `iex` verbs, never a substring. Nested forms are
     evaluated to a depth of 4; the fifth level denies `unprovable-destruction`.
   - Case-insensitive comparison applies only to these membership tests.
2. **Invariant.** No verb or form added in item 1 turns a command denied before this change into an allowed
   one. The invariant covers item 1 only, not item 5. Item 5 makes two denials into allows on purpose, both in an
   Antigravity `run_command` with `Cwd`: the resolved-`Cwd` base (`rm -rf ../../x` with `Cwd` `<ws>/a/b` now
   resolves to `<ws>/x`, inside the project), and the unresolvable `Cwd` (`cat .env` with `Cwd` `$PWD`, denied
   `secret-access` before and allowed now, because the operand becomes the unresolved `$X/.env`; `cat $PWD/.env`
   already passed before, gap G-1).
3. **`policy-surface-write` and `history-rewrite` do not change**, not even in case: a `CAT` naming the policy
   surface stays denied as before.
4. **`FETCH_VERBS` and the verb list `fetchedProgramReachesShell` inspects do not change.** `iex` and `irm` are
   decided by head and first name only. Adding them to that test would reach neither `iex ([string](irm …))`
   nor a fetch verb inside a script block, which stay gap G-16.
5. **The wiring-tamper routes close for Antigravity only, through an optional port.** `ProviderPort` gains
   `floorHostFacts?(event, protectedPaths)`, returning the neutral `FloorHostFacts` of `src/contracts/`:
   textual wiring names, the strings of a tool outside the translation table, the resolved or unresolvable shell
   base, wiring file names, protected ancestors, case folding and the canonical form. `run.ts` asks for them
   inside the handler's `try`, so a throw is `handler-error`. With them the floor denies `wiring-tamper` for an
   untranslated tool whose arguments name a hooks.json file at any depth; resolves relative operands against
   the resolved `Cwd` for every rule; with an unresolvable `Cwd` denies a command naming `hooks.json`, a
   relative destructive operand (`unprovable-destruction`) and a relative move or rename operand
   (`wiring-tamper`); denies destroying or moving a protected ancestor; compares protected targets without case;
   and on Windows compares them in the canonical form of `antigravityCanonicalPath`. Protected ancestors are
   every directory that strictly contains a protected target, except the project root, the home and a
   filesystem root. Cursor and Claude keep the previous behaviour on these routes (gap G-12); this decision does
   not extend them.
6. **A launcher path with a space is still refused**, and the reason now says why:
   `Quoting does not help on agy 1.2.14: the host splits the hook command on spaces, a quote stays a literal character in the argument, and the hook runs with its working directory set to the hooks.json directory.`
   Measured on agy 1.2.14 on 2026-09-30, including under `cmd.exe /d /s /c`. The install does not emit quotes.
7. **`harness_floor` stays `false`.** The global group in `~/.gemini/config/hooks.json` was measured with a
   subagent on agy 1.2.14: only the parent's tools fired (`define_subagent`, `invoke_subagent`, two `Stop`); the
   subagent's `view_file` and `send_message` fired neither `PreToolUse` nor `PostToolUse`.
8. **Declared limitations, not missing work.** CLI 1.2.14 in print mode did not offer
   `multi_replace_file_content` in two sessions and did not load a configured MCP server (`MCP_UNAVAILABLE`);
   the IDE 2.0.2 and the app 2.18.1 made no tool call. The `multi_replace_file_content` fixture stays synthetic
   and `verified: false`.
9. **The host's user documents.** `userSettingsPaths()` lists `~/.gemini/config/hooks.json` last, because that
   document keeps the group under a named key and never makes a project shim stand down. `providerConfigDirs()`
   gains `~/.gemini/antigravity-cli`, so the install links `harness-init` into its `skills/` and the doctor
   checks that link. The uninstall removes that link when it is the installer's, and nothing else in the
   directory.
10. **The gate passes with the default Windows `%TEMP%`: the test is fixed, not documented.** The one failure
    was the test asserting that no derived path starts with the real home, and the default `%TEMP%` lies inside
    the real home on every Windows machine. The assertion now targets what its title protects, the real
    provider directories; the fake-home guards beside it still hold.
11. **Only the upstream repository releases.** The `decide` and `release` jobs of `release.yml` carry
    `github.repository == 'tech-leads-club/harness-toolkit'`, so a push to `main` of a fork never reaches
    `npm publish`.
12. **AGH-47 of the `tlc-harness-antigravity` feature is retired.** It held the floor's decision files to no diff
    against a base, and item 1 changes the floor on purpose. The AGH-80 tests still bar the host's `Stop` event
    token from core, and no longer count `Stop-Computer`, which item 1 brings into the floor catalog.

## Why

An independent audit of the Antigravity provider ([/decisions/ad-156.md](/decisions/ad-156.md)) left an agent on
Windows able to delete, read a secret, shut the machine down or run downloaded code through a cmdlet, and left
two wiring-tamper routes open on Antigravity. The measurements on agy 1.2.14 decided the rest: quoting is not
shell quoting, the global group does not fire inside a subagent, and print mode offers neither the multi-edit
tool nor MCP. The port keeps the host facts out of core, the same way the canonical form of agentic-squad
decisions AD-049 and AD-050 reached the write tools.

## Trade-offs

- A POSIX grouping `{ rm -rf /tmp/x; }` is now denied `unprovable-destruction` by the script-block rule, and so
  is a literal `{` in a word without a leading quote.
- A project script `./format` is denied `outside-project-destruction`.
- Positional `powershell` tries every suffix: `powershell -NoProfile Write-Host del` is denied.
- The tokenizer does not keep which quote opened a word: `git commit -m 'use $(rm) x'` is denied.
- With an unresolvable `Cwd`, `Move-Item a.txt b.txt` is denied `wiring-tamper`; the remedy is a literal `Cwd`.
- The split form holds with any separator: `iex "("; irm https://example.invalid/x` is denied although nothing
  downloaded runs.
- Short names collide with POSIX tools (`ri`, `gc`, `type`, `start`); only false positives follow.

## Not decided here

- The gaps stay gaps:
  - G-1: a shell path built from an environment variable is not resolved (`$env:`, `$HOME`).
  - G-2: a download handed to `Invoke-Expression` by a means that is not a fetch verb is not recognized
    (`DownloadString`, `Start-BitsTransfer`).
  - G-3: the content of a script run by `-File` or `Invoke-Command -FilePath` is not inspected.
  - G-4: `Get-Content ./x.ps1 | iex`, and downloading to a file and then running it, are allowed.
  - G-5: the arguments of `Start-Process` (`-ArgumentList`) and a parameter value before the executable are not
    inspected.
  - G-6: .NET methods (`[IO.File]`), WMI/CIM (`Invoke-CimMethod`) and COM objects are not recognized.
  - G-7: a tool outside the translation table is checked only by the `textual` name of the two hooks.json files.
  - G-8: the `Cwd` base applies only to `run_command`; with an unresolvable `Cwd`, a read or write through a
    relative operand of a verb that neither destroys nor moves, in a command not naming `hooks.json`, is not
    refused.
  - G-9: the project root is not a protected ancestor, and a glob operand is literal text (`.\*`).
  - G-10: indirection through an alias, a function, a variable or a computed name is not recognized
    (`Set-Alias`, `'Re'+'move-Item'`).
  - G-11: a writer reaching a hooks.json file through its directory without being a destructive, move or rename
    verb is not refused (`robocopy`, `Expand-Archive`).
  - G-12: in a Cursor or Claude session, case, protected ancestors, textual names, `Cwd` and the shell-route
    canonical form do not apply (`.AGENTS`).
  - G-13: the `\\?\UNC\` prefix and hardlinks are not resolved.
  - G-14: a directory change inside the command (`cd`, `chdir`, `Set-Location`, `sl`, `Push-Location`, `pushd`)
    does not change the base of the following segments.
  - G-15: a directory link created in one segment and used in a following one is not resolved (`mklink`).
  - G-16: a fetch verb in the argument of `Invoke-Expression` is recognized only as a first name of the `iex`
    segment's words or as the head of the segment after one ending in `(`; `iex ([string](irm …))`,
    `iex (& { irm … })` and `iex $(& { irm … })` are not refused.
- Quoting or any other way to accept a launcher path with a space.
- `harness_floor: true` for Antigravity.
- Real captures of `multi_replace_file_content`, of an MCP tool, and of `Stop`/`PostToolUse` on the IDE and the
  app.
